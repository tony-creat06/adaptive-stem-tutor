# Longitudinal learning evidence

## Product goal and claim boundary

The goal is to help the learner understand more deeply, retrieve knowledge
after time has passed, select methods in unfamiliar problems, and express
answers effectively under the conditions of the supplied assessment. These are
controllable teaching targets that can plausibly support higher marks.

Do not promise a mark increase, claim that the tutor caused a score change, or
predict the content of an unseen future paper. A future official result depends
on many factors outside the tutor, and future questions do not yet exist as
evidence. Improve the learning process and collect honest performance evidence
instead.

## Evidence ladder

Keep the following claims separate:

- `observed_once`: correct or substantially correct performance in the current
  context;
- `transfer_observed`: independent use of the same criterion in a meaningfully
  changed context;
- `retained_later`: independent success on an unseen changed-context retrieval
  after a scheduled delay;
- `integrated`: successful coordination with other objectives in a cumulative
  or multi-part task;
- `observed assessment change`: the arithmetic difference between protected
  baseline and posttest forms under matched conditions;
- real exam improvement: not established unless an actual comparable exam
  result is supplied, and still not a causal claim without an appropriate study
  design.

Reading, recognition, copying a worked example, immediate repetition, and
self-reported confidence cannot establish retention. A state transition or a
green regression test cannot establish learner benefit.

## Delayed changed-context retrieval

After transfer is observed or a chapter readiness review succeeds, schedule a
later retrieval rather than declaring durable mastery. A valid retrieval plan
records:

- the criterion and objective being revisited;
- the time of the prior evidence;
- a due time at least one full day later;
- how the surface context or representation will change;
- a stable local retrieval identifier.

Use the interval to fit the learner and course schedule. A short revisit after
two or three days and a later cumulative revisit after one or two weeks are
useful defaults, not universal mastery thresholds. Intervening study can itself
provide the delay when the dates remain explicit.

At the due turn:

1. do not show the worked solution, relevant notes, or leading hints first;
2. use a task the learner has not seen before the attempt;
3. require an independent response in the learner's normal answer form;
4. record the result, including an error rather than silently rescheduling it;
5. repair locally, then schedule another genuinely later retrieval if needed.

Record `retained_later` only when the state tool accepts the scheduled link,
the due time has passed, the attempt was independent, and the task was unseen
before the attempt. Otherwise use a weaker or uncertain judgment.

## Protected baseline and posttest

A useful product pilot can measure observed performance change without claiming
causation. Before targeted teaching, select a protected baseline form. After
the teaching and delayed retrieval cycle, use a distinct protected posttest
form.

Both attempts must be:

- held out and unseen before the attempt;
- solution-blind;
- independent;
- timed, with the actual duration recorded;
- scored using the stated marks and the same declared interpretation rules;
- linked to the same protocol but different form identifiers;
- comparable in objective coverage and overall demand.

Record enough conditions to interpret the result, including interruptions,
omissions, permitted aids, and any material deviation. Do not store full learner
answers by default.

Report the numerical result as, for example, “the protected posttest was 15
percentage points higher than the protected baseline under these recorded
conditions.” Follow immediately with the boundary: this is an observed change,
not proof that the tutor caused the change and not a guarantee of the official
exam result.

If forms differ substantially, solutions were exposed, timing differs, or the
learner received hints, do not present a baseline/posttest comparison as clean
evidence. Preserve the attempt as practice evidence with the limitation.

## Unknown future papers: robustness, not prediction

Use past papers to learn stable demands, course conventions, answer forms, and
the range of ways objectives have been assessed. Do not train only on repeated
surface topics or convert frequency into a future-paper promise.

When several years are available, use a leave-one-year-out audit:

1. hide one year's paper and solution from the planning set;
2. build the coverage and practice plan from primary teaching material plus the
   remaining years;
3. reveal the held-out paper only for evaluation;
4. map which held-out objectives, demand types, and unfamiliar contexts the
   plan would have prepared;
5. record genuinely novel or missed demands;
6. repeat for each usable year.

Passing means the plan remains syllabus-complete and teaches transferable
method selection across the held-out demands. It does not mean the hidden year
or a future paper was predicted. A held-out miss is a reason to improve
coverage, representation switching, integration, or practice diversity—not to
invent a more confident forecast.

## Adaptation rules

Adapt from cumulative evidence, not confidence or one answer:

- repeated conceptual errors: restore the smallest missing prerequisite and
  change the explanation or representation;
- correct current-context work but weak transfer: add changed-context method
  selection before increasing speed;
- transfer success but failed delayed retrieval: shorten the next interval,
  use retrieval rather than rereading, and revisit the exact criterion;
- accurate but slow work: preserve the method and add bounded timed fluency;
- fast work with lost assumptions, units, signs, or explanation marks: train
  an exam-ready completion checklist;
- broad cumulative success: increase integration, paper breadth, and timing
  while continuing spaced retrieval of earlier objectives.

Never lower the criterion merely to create positive-looking progress. Never
hold the learner on already secure foundations when cumulative evidence supports
advancing.

## Learner-facing communication

Tell the learner what the evidence supports in plain language. Good examples:

- “你刚才在新情境中独立选对了方法；这支持迁移，但还不能说明长期保持。”
- “我们隔了七天换了题面，你仍能独立完成，所以现在有一次延迟提取证据。”
- “这次限时后测比基线高 15 个百分点；这是本次条件下观察到的变化，不是对正式考试成绩的保证。”

Do not expose protocol identifiers, state filenames, internal timestamps, or
claim labels in ordinary teaching.
