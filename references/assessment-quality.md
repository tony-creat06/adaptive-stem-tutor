# Assessment and past-paper quality

## Purpose

Past papers reveal what the learner must be able to produce under assessment
conditions. They do not replace the syllabus, lecture notes, or chapter
objectives. Use them to build an assessment bridge from course knowledge to
independent exam performance.

## Audit the corpus before teaching to the exam

When past papers are available:

1. inventory every supplied paper and solution by the academic year stated
   inside the document, not by filename alone;
2. pair each question paper with its solution or mark scheme by content;
3. record duration, permitted aids, compulsory/optional structure, section
   structure, marks, and other conditions only when the supplied paper states
   them;
4. map every question and substantial subpart to course objectives and to its
   principal demand: recall, explain, derive, calculate, model, estimate,
   sketch, interpret, compare, or evaluate;
5. inspect formula-critical and layout-dependent pages visually;
6. assess the solution independently for completeness, legibility, alignment
   with the question, correctness, and pedagogical sufficiency.

Do not call the audit complete because text extraction succeeded. A scanned
solution with no text layer requires visual inspection. A solution can be
`reviewed` as a source while still being defective or unsuitable for direct
teaching; record that distinction in its authority note and assessment map.

## Authority and recency

Use the newest supplied paper as the strongest evidence of the newest supplied
format, then seek corroboration from adjacent years. Older papers remain
valuable for content, integration, and transfer, but their timing or structure
may be obsolete.

Never infer an unstated duration, calculator rule, formula-sheet rule, optional
choice, or current format from another year. If the most recent paper is not
confirmed as the learner's actual assessment year, say “newest supplied
format,” not “the current exam.”

Past-paper frequency is not syllabus authority. A topic absent from the sample
still remains in the course when the primary teaching source includes it.

## Assessment map

When assessment sources exist, maintain `assessment-map.md` in the course
workspace. It should contain:

- paper year and exact supplied format evidence;
- question-by-question chapter/objective mapping;
- marks and answer form;
- principal reasoning demand and prerequisite chain;
- repeated, newly appearing, and unsampled objectives;
- solution availability, review state, and defects;
- which format is used for timed practice and why.

The map is evidence, not a prediction of future questions. Do not turn topic
frequency into a probability claim unless the available corpus and method can
support it.

When several years are available, test the plan by holding out one year at a
time. Build the route from primary teaching material and the remaining years,
then reveal the hidden paper and record missed objectives, demand types, and
contexts. Use this to improve syllabus coverage and transfer diversity. Never
describe a successful held-out audit as prediction of a future paper; follow
`learning-evidence.md`.

## Exam-readiness progression

Build practice in layers:

1. **chapter foundations** — definitions, representations, and standard
   procedures without time pressure;
2. **chapter exam bridge** — concise explanations and derivations in the
   course's real answer form with visible mark allocation;
3. **changed-context transfer** — an unseen surface context that requires the
   same criterion and method selection;
4. **cumulative mixed retrieval** — earlier chapters return after intervening
   material;
5. **timed section practice** — reproduce a verified supplied section format;
6. **full-paper rehearsal** — use only a format and duration supported by the
   chosen paper, then review time allocation, method selection, and lost marks.

A chapter can be ready to transition without being exam-ready. Exam readiness
requires evidence across recall, derivation, explanation, calculation, and
unfamiliar multi-part transfer as demanded by the supplied assessment corpus.

For a longitudinal product pilot, keep one timed baseline form protected before
targeted teaching and use a distinct, comparably demanding protected posttest
form afterward. Both must be held-out, unseen, solution-blind, independent, and
recorded under matched declared conditions. Report any percentage-point
difference as an observed change rather than a causal tutoring effect or a
promise about the official exam.

## Marking and feedback

Before revealing a solution, require an authentic attempt unless the learner
explicitly requests direct explanation. Mark against the question, stated
assumptions, course conventions, and available allocation—not phrase matching.

After an attempt:

- separate method marks, execution marks, and explanation marks when the
  source supports that distinction;
- identify the earliest lost-mark decision;
- show a concise exam-ready answer after teaching the reusable method;
- check units, signs, limits, sketches, assumptions, and physical meaning;
- do not treat a model solution as infallible when it is incomplete,
  misordered, internally corrected, or mismatched to the paper;
- independently verify questionable mathematics against primary course
  material before using it to judge the learner.

Never copy a terse mark scheme and call it teaching. Never expand a defective
solution into a confident answer without resolving the defect.
