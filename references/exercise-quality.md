# Exercise, solution, and teaching-material quality

## Exercise coverage

For every chapter or teaching unit, compare its objectives with the available
tasks. When suitable, distinguish:

- foundational: checks one definition, representation, or standard procedure;
- transfer: changes the surface context while preserving the criterion;
- integrative: combines several objectives and requires method selection;
- exam-style: reflects the course's real answer form, depth, and time pressure.

`exam-style` must be supported by an audited assessment source when past papers
exist. Follow `assessment-quality.md` for question-level mapping, format
recency, timing, and solution defects. Do not use chapter problem sheets alone
to claim exam readiness.

Official status does not guarantee complete coverage or good sequencing. Record
objectives that have no suitable task. A generated supplement is allowed when it
is derivable from reviewed sources, but label it as tutor-generated and do not
present it as an official problem or past-paper question.

Prefer a small progression over a large undifferentiated worksheet. A Check
after teaching should not be a numerical clone or a noun-swapped clone of the
worked example. Compare system, operation, representation, inference direction,
constraints, and required output; change at least two meaningful dimensions for
an immediate transfer Check. A closer rehearsal is appropriate only inside a
bounded repair and must be followed by an independent changed-context retry.

## Combined problem and solution files

Many course PDFs reveal a question and its solution on the same or adjacent
pages. Treat the question scope as `practice` and the answer scope as
`solution_reference` under distinct source IDs, even when both records share one
physical path and hash. If page boundaries cannot prevent leakage, transcribe or
paraphrase only the question needed for the current attempt and keep the PDF
private until after the attempt.

## Reviewing a learner attempt

Use the solution reference to verify course conventions and marking expectations,
then produce answer-specific feedback:

1. preserve the learner's valid method;
2. identify the earliest consequential gap;
3. expose the decision or principle that repairs it;
4. show enough intermediate work to make the method reusable;
5. check sign, units, limit, normalization, boundary conditions, or physical
   meaning where relevant;
6. use an independent retry when evidence is still needed.

Do not dump or lightly paraphrase the official solution. A terse mark scheme may
be sufficient for grading evidence but insufficient for teaching; add a
source-consistent pedagogical bridge and identify it privately as tutor
explanation.

If a model solution contains an answer that does not correspond to the paired
paper, visible corrections, missing parts, or suspicious mathematics, quarantine
that part from automatic grading until it is checked against the question and
primary course source. “Official” and “reviewed” describe provenance and review,
not correctness or teaching quality.

## Auditing lecture notes as teaching sources

Lecture notes establish course content, but they are not automatically a good
lesson. Before relying on a section, inspect:

- its motivating question or physical purpose;
- whether intuition and representations precede dense formalism when needed;
- derivation steps that the notes omit or compress;
- notation, assumptions, and changes of basis or coordinates;
- worked examples and the decisions they expose;
- diagrams or layout that text extraction cannot preserve;
- alignment between stated objectives, examples, exercises, and assessments;
- edition mismatches and material that is only briefly surveyed.

The tutor may add analogies, intermediate algebra, diagrams, contrasting cases,
and prerequisite bridges from safe general knowledge. Do not attribute those
additions to the source. Keep the learner-facing explanation coherent and
natural instead of replaying slide bullets.
